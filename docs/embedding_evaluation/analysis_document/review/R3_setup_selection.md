# R3 review: §2 Setup selection

Reviewed 2026-09-30 against setup_selection/ (SETUP_SELECTION.md, every CSV), registers_check/ (REGISTERS_CHECK.md, CSVs, figure), EVTOLNEWS_DS/2_embedding_extraction/attention/registers_share.csv, heldout_selection.py, preprocess_variants_eval.py, preprocess_variants.py (extraction), registers_experiment.py, and CHAPTER_PLAN.md §3, N2 and §4b. Nothing was changed. Every number in §2 matches its CSV. The problems are in how the numbers are framed, in two measurement details, and in three missing controls.

Checked and sound: the split is by maker, and every aircraft follows its maker. Neighbours are drawn only within the half, and the own maker is also left out inside the half. The winner on A is scored on B and the reverse (select() in heldout_selection.py). There are 200 splits with seed 42, which gives 400 choices. The registers share in Table 9 (70.0 / 67.8 / 89.5 %) is the sum of the six registers rows of s1a_candidates.csv. The grid comes before the rule, the rule before the results, and the chosen-setup table closes the section.

**F3.1 [MUST]** · §2 intro, Table 7 row "model", §2.2 last paragraph, Table 13 · The text says the model was "fixed in advance on the attention artifact". The candidate grid and the decision rule are also presented as fixed before any label score was seen. The record says otherwise. The full-sample layer grid under base was known from the 2026-09-22 reports, where the in-sample layer rule picked L24 CLS in 96 % of subsets. The registers scores (registers_knn.csv 00:49 and REGISTERS_CHECK.md 01:22 on 30 Sept) showed +0.057 and +0.111 on the drawings. CHAPTER_PLAN N2 then reads "Model decision deferred ... Decide by comparison". The midday ruling came after that, and the split-half run followed at 12:25. So the split-half rule confirmed choices already seen in-sample. It did not make them blind. · Why it matters: this is the first thing a methods jury will ask ("when did you decide, and what had you seen?"). The heldout_selection.py docstring says "fixed before the numbers were seen", which is true only of the split-half numbers. · What to do: add a three-line timeline to §2.1: reports 09-22 (in-sample grid), registers check 09-30 night (full-sample comparison), ruling and split-half run 09-30 midday. Reword the model row to: "motivated by a label-free diagnostic (attention collapse), adopted after the full-sample comparison, confirmed by the split-half rule". Because the model choice saw labels, treat it as label-influenced. Quote the all-12 optimism gap (0.018 to 0.038) as the headline gap, not the registers-only gap (0.014 to 0.021). · Cost: text only.

**F3.2 [MUST]** · §2 intro sentence "a choice made on labels must not be scored on the aircraft that made it"; §2.6 · §4 to §6 then report full-sample scores (0.235, 0.445, 0.563) on exactly the aircraft that made the choice. The split-half rule only shows that the choice holds up held out. The headline numbers are still post-selection. §2.6 calls the gap "a few hundredths at most ... small against the intervals". Measured against the signal, it is not small. On 12 classes, 0.027 (all-12 pool) is about 19 % of the margin above the shuffled level (0.235 minus 0.096). On the drawings' 5 classes, 0.038 is about 16 % of the margin (0.445 minus 0.214). On the photos it is about 5 %. · Why it matters: the intro sentence promises something the document does not deliver. A reader who spots this will distrust the whole section. · What to do: (a) Reword the intro to "a choice made on labels is confirmed on makers that did not make it, and its optimism is reported". (b) In §2.6, say plainly that §4 to §6 are full-sample scores carrying an optimism of about 0.02 to 0.04. Give the gap also as a share of the above-chance margin. (c) Beside each headline in Table 1, give the held-out estimate of the chosen setup (0.164 / 0.318 / 0.535, all-12 pool, s1a_labels.csv), or a gap-corrected value. · Cost: already in saved results.

**F3.3 [MUST]** · Table 7, §2.2, Table 13 row "model", §0 Table 2 "image type", §8 recipe · Registers is labelled "image type: drawings are mostly white". Table 8 contradicts this. The base collapse is the same on photos (L24 0.280) as on drawings (0.295), and the collapsed patch is the white pad that our own preprocessing adds to both sources. The collapse is a property of large DINOv2 at 518 px (Darcet et al.), not of patent drawings. More importantly, the only evidence that the collapse harms the vector is label evidence. On the same halves that evidence is weaker than the full-sample CI suggests: registers minus base is +0.016 / +0.030 / +0.020, and registers is higher in only 67 / 68 / 79 % of halves (sd 0.031 on 12 classes). After the view erasure the two models tie (§4.4). The 37 % to 48 % matching gain rests on 94 queries and has no interval. · Why it matters: the author's rule is "image-type-justified without labels". As written, label evidence is doing the justifying under an image-type heading. · What to do: rename the kind "model property, label-free diagnostic, label-confirmed". State the reusable rule as a test anyone can run without labels: "if the median max-patch share of the late-layer CLS attention exceeds about 0.1 on your images, use the registers checkpoint". In Table 13, show the held-out differences and the % of halves beside the full-sample paired CIs. Add the §4.4 tie to the reason column. Optionally, add a label-free embedding-level check, such as the share of images whose argmax patch lies in the pad (CPU, from the saved attention arrays and the pad boxes) or patch-token norm outliers (GPU). · Cost: text; CPU for the pad-location share; GPU only for token norms.

**F3.4 [MUST]** · §2.4, Table 11, Figure 6, §8 recipe ("leave the lines as drawn") · The thick variants are not a clean test of stroke width. (a) _prepare_one converts every thick copy to greyscale, while "plain" keeps colour. So for the 210 colour drawings, thickening is confounded with colour removal. (b) A minimum filter on a greyscale scan turns faint anti-aliased strokes into solid black and floods halftone and hatching. Figure 6 shows this in the SLC and HB rows. thick1 is therefore a large contrast and fill change, not a "1 px" nudge. (c) The square window grows diagonal strokes by about √2 times the radius. (d) 5 small figures stay unfiltered in thick1. · Why it matters: the conclusion "thickening fills fine detail" and the §8 recipe generalise beyond what was tested. The one gentle variant that would support the recipe was never run, which §9 already admits. · What to do: add two controls. First, "plain grey", which isolates the colour loss. Second, "contrast only", which darkens strokes to black without dilation and isolates the darkening. Then restate the result as "a dilation of one input pixel per side or more lowers the score; a gentler one is untested", and limit the §8 recipe to that. Add the per-half evidence, which is stronger than the CIs: thick2 is worse than plain in 88 to 91 % of halves, thick1 in 64 to 73 % (s1b_scores.csv). Say how the 3 rows of Figure 6 were picked from the 8, so the montage is not read as cherry-picked. · Cost: GPU for 2 variants × about 1.5 k figures (small), then CPU evaluation; per-half numbers are already saved.

**F3.6 [SHOULD]** · Table 7 (absent), §1.2, §4.5 · The largest swing in the drawing score is the image rule. exp3 main figure scores 0.235, while exp1, exp2 and exp4 score 0.195 / 0.194 / 0.166. The kNN k = 5 and the cosine metric are also fixed protocol choices that do not appear in the grid. · Why it matters: a jury will see that the primary rule is also the best-scoring rule and ask whether that is a coincidence. · What to do: either document the date and source of the "exp3 = primary" ruling, or run exp1 to exp4 through the same split-half rule. **F3.7 [SHOULD]** · §2.1 decision rule, §2.3, §2.5, Table 10 · Three issues with how the rule is stated. (a) The rule is based on selection stability. It never says that the performance criterion (best mean held-out score as a fixed candidate) picks the same setup. It does: best_fixed is registers L24 CLS for all three label sets, and plain / colour for the preparations. That is the strongest robustness sentence available, and it is missing. (b) The aggregate frequency hides how unstable single choices are. The two halves of a split pick the same candidate in only 37 to 65 % of splits (registers pool) and 28 to 40 % (all 12). On photos, L24 CLS minus L22 CLS held out is +0.005 and positive in 59 % of halves, which is a tie. (c) The tie clause has no margin, so colour against grey (57 / 43 %, +0.006 [-0.012, +0.026]) is formally "label-selected" although the rule is indifferent. The rule's step of averaging over the two sources is also a design constraint (one readout for both, for §6), and it is not stated as such. With that constraint, the drawings decide the photos' layer. · Why it matters: these are the first questions about a frequency-based rule, and the answers are already on disk. · What to do: add the "both criteria agree" sentence. Declare a practical-equivalence margin (for example, frequency within 60 / 40 or held-out difference below 0.01 means keep the default or untransformed option), and relabel photo preparation "default kept, rule indifferent". State the one-readout constraint as a rule before the numbers, and give each source's own pick (drawings L24 CLS 77 to 79 %; photos L24 CLS 60 %, L22 CLS 40 %). · Cost: already in saved results.

**F3.8 [SHOULD]** · Table 9, Table 11, Table 13 wording "interval below zero" · The seven paired contrasts in §2 are reported with unadjusted 95 % intervals. Several sit on the edge: registers +0.008 lower bound, thick2 upper bounds -0.005 and -0.013. In addition, _boot_diff resamples aircraft with the predictions held fixed. It ignores both maker clustering (one maker can contribute many aircraft) and the variability of the neighbour pool, so the intervals are too narrow. · Why it matters: the "interval excludes zero" claims would likely not survive a Holm correction or a maker-level bootstrap. The per-half evidence is sturdier, and it is not used. · What to do: rerun the paired bootstrap resampling makers (CPU), or add a Holm note. Lead with the per-half consistency (share of halves where the difference has the same sign) rather than "excludes zero". · Cost: CPU.

**F3.9 [SHOULD]** · §2.1 · The mechanics are under-described. (a) Halves are unbalanced (248 to 415 drawing aircraft) because makers differ in size. (b) Rare classes (DS 5, SRW 9, RC 9, PFV 10) can be missing from a half. The code silences this warning, and balanced accuracy is then averaged over fewer classes, so the 12-class half score has a chance level that moves from half to half. (c) The A and B choices of one split are dependent, so the 400 choices are not 400 independent draws. The Monte Carlo SE of a frequency near 69 % is about 3 points. (d) Only one split seed was used. · Why it matters: each of these is cheap to answer and costly to leave open. · What to do: one sentence each. Report how many halves miss one or more classes. Rerun with two further seeds and show that the frequencies move by only a few points. Give the Monte Carlo SE. · Cost: CPU (minutes).
